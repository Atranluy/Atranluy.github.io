Whole-genome reveal Phylogeographic, Demographic and Adapation patterns in the Indo-Pacific Bottlenose dolphins Tursiops aduncus

I collaborate with Dr. Svenja Marfurt on her PhD project on Tursiops aduncus. Using whole-genome sequencing of individuals sampled along most of the Australian coastline, we examined population structure and adaptive divergence at a continental scale. Genome-wide variation tracked geography, consistent with a northern origin followed by two independent stepping-stone colonisations down the eastern and western coasts. Putatively adaptive loci, in contrast, clustered individuals by tropical waters regardless of geographic distance, indicating parallel adaptation and a tropical–temperate, potentially ecotype-like differentiation relevant to conservation planning under climate change.

Article in Molecular ecology, Marfurt et al., 2026 - 10.1111/mec.70383

Speciation history among Tursiops species along the Australian coastline using Whole-genome

This is an ongoing project involving Whole-genome, sex chromosomes and mtDNA to fully resolve the speciation history of T. adunus, T. truncatus and T. australis around Australia coastline. This project is motivated by: (I) some T. aduncus display the mtDNA haplotype of T. truncatus or T. australis in specific locations while in autosomes they show no sign of introgression. (II) Resolving the divergence history of T. australis with T. truncatus by reusing WG of coastal/pelagic ecotype of T. truncatus from Louis et al., (2021).

Sousa sahulensis genomics using low-coverage whole-genome sequencing

I collaborate with Dr. Chabanne on this project exploring the population structure in the Australian humpback dolphin (Sousa sahulensis), using low-coverage whole-genome sequencing.
46 individuals sampled across five localities spanning more than 1,000 km of northwestern Australia. We found two highly differentiated populations separated by a genetic break between Port Hedland and Cygnet Bay, with related individuals clustering strongly within localities, indicating very limited dispersal. Inbreeding was detected in both populations but with contrasting signatures. Long runs of homozygosity in the southwestern population suggesting recent inbreeding, and short runs in the northeastern population consistent with an older bottleneck and small effective population size. These results highlight strong subdivision and isolation in this elusive species, underscoring the need for conservation management.
