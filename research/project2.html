Comparative population genomics and phylogeography in the Western Pacific

During my PhD, I studied the Western Pacific hydrothermal fauna to understand the origins of its genetic diversity and connectivity as a distinct biogeographic region. To achieve this, we sampled the dominant fauna during the Chubacarc 2019 cruise. Using a comparative ddRAD sequencing approach, we genotyped seven key species: Ifremeria, Alviniconcha, Bathymodiolus, Branchinotogluma Eochionelasmus, Lepetodrilus, , and Shinkailepas . All species were collected from identical locations using consistent methodologies. I first analyzed population structure and genetic diversity patterns across the region, then reconstructed their demographic histories—accounting for genomic heterogeneity—to infer evolutionary and divergence patterns across this bioregion.

Comparative article (MBE, 2025) - 10.1093/molbev/msaf024

Genetic Connectivity, subtilities and limits

This work comprises two independent studies of population connectivity in hydrothermal vent fauna, addressing complementary spatial scales. The first examines the gastropod Ifremeria nautilei across the back-arc basins of the western Pacific; the second is on a single copepod species Stygiopontius lauensis within the Lau Basin. Both employ population genomic analyses and inferences to characterise patterns of gene flow among localities and populations.

While we found some genetic connectivity among populations, complementary analyses reveal limits to it. The weak or not observed genetic differentiation accross localities, is attributable to a combination of low but existing gene flow and likely large effective population sizes, reducing the genetic drift. Indeed, Analyses of outlier loci recover subtle constraints on this connectivity and revealing a limit of demographic connectivity across localities and populations.

Population genetic on Ifremeria nautilei (Mol ecol, 2022) - 10.1111/mec.16430
Population genetic on Stygiopontius lauensis (Mol ecol, 2024) - 10.1111/mec.17340

Small collaboration on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge

Here, I collaborated on Dr. Elodi portanier project, contributing to the demographic inferences for two hydrothermal vent gastropods, Lepetodrilus atlanticus and Peltospira smaragdina, along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. Using more than 15,000 SNPs and mtCox1 sequences, she showed that each species is fragmented into three distinct genetic groups, with a semipermeable zone south of Rainbow (35°N) suggesting a multispecies hybrid zone. Demographic modelling supported secondary contact between most metapopulation pairs, indicating gene flow that is rare or constrained by genetic barriers — a fragmentation with direct implications for conservation as deep-sea mining targets the ridge.

Conservation implications of low contemporary connectivity along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge in hydrothermal vent gastropods (Conservation Biology, 2026) - 10.1111/cobi.70284
