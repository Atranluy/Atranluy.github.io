Cryptic diversity and speciation continuum

Through international collaboration, we analyzed whole genomes of 144 cold-water coral individuals across four species. Our findings reveal thatMadrepora oculata ,long considered cosmopolitan—comprises multiple cryptic lineages. Meanwhile, Desmophyllum pertusum and D.dianthus show no introgression despite sympatry, confirming their status as distinct sister species. Comparative analyses suggest these corals may initiate speciation at lower genomic divergence than most animals—patterns more comparable to plants than typical marine invertebrates. These results call for a systematic genomic revision of cold-water coral taxonomy to improve conservation strategies and diversity assessements.

Phylogeographic Pattern of Desmophyllum pertusum (Lophelia pertusa)

Using 182 whole genomes, we produced the first comprehensive view of the genetic diversity and connectivity in this flagship reef-building coral across the Atlantic. Population structure mirrors the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC), while regional patterns reveal contact zones between local lineages, shaped by glacial cycles. Evidence suggests post-Last Glacial Maximum recolonization from southern refugia, highlighting how spatial genetic diversity informs conservation and restoration priorities.
